## 3. GROSS MARGIN & MARKUP NORMS — APPAREL DTC

### Industry Standard Multipliers

| Channel Model | Typical COGS Multiplier | Gross Margin % | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast fashion / mass market | 1.5–2.0x (keystone) | 33–50% | H&M, Decathlon tier |
| Wholesale (brand to retailer) | 2.0x | ~50% | Standard "keystone" wholesale |
| Premium DTC (brand to consumer direct) | 2.5–4.0x | 60–75% | Lululemon, premium local brands |
| Super-premium DTC (brand-perceived luxury) | 4.0–6.0x | 75–83% | Alo Yoga, PE Nation |
| Realistic PH premium DTC (B Active target) | 2.5–3.5x | 60–71% | Must account for COD losses, higher logistics |

**Benchmark examples in PH context:**
- Recess (local premium): If COGS ~₱350–₱450 on a ₱949–₱1,199 legging, that's ~2.2–2.7x (est. 55–63% GM). Consistent with premium local DTC.
- MILLENNX at ₱1,980: If COGS ~₱550–₱700, that's 2.8–3.6x (est. 65–72% GM). Premium positioning.
- Bench at ₱1,090 tennis skirt: Lower GM likely — Bench is a mass-market PH brand using volume model, probably 2.0–2.3x.
- Nike PH at ₱5,795: Nike's global gross margin ~46% retail; their landed PH cost is high due to import duties + distribution.

### Philippine-Specific Margin Adjustments

DTC in PH is structurally harder than Western DTC due to:
1. **COD dominance** = 68% of PH online shoppers prefer COD, which means you carry the float and absorb all RTO losses.
2. **Higher RTO costs** = A ₱1,000 COD order that bounces costs ₱150–₱250 round-trip shipping + your COGS risk on returned goods.
3. **No Stripe-level efficiency** = Payment processing averages 2.0–3.5% vs. 2.9% flat in US.

**Practical rule:** In PH DTC, budget for effective gross margin of 55–65% (after payment fees, shipping subsidy, and a COD RTO provision) — not the 65–75% that looks clean on paper.

## 4. PHILIPPINE PRICE PSYCHOLOGY

### Key Price Thresholds for Women Shoppers (₱)

| Price Point | What It Feels Like | Implication for B Active |
|---|---|---|
| Under ₱500 | "Shopee budget buy" — disposable, low expectation | Avoid; below brand floor |
| ₱500–₱999 | "Affordable basics" — H&M, Bench, Decathlon territory | Entry-level only; not premium |
| ₱1,000 | **First psychological barrier** — feels like "spending money" | ₱999 is a classic sub-barrier price (₱X99 convention); ₱1,090 is "over ₱1,000" |
| ₱1,000–₱1,999 | "Mid-range local premium" — Recess, Andi, MILLENNX, Bench Active zone | Core B Active target range for separates |
| ₱2,000 | **Second barrier** — entering "investment purchase" territory | ₱1,990 or ₱1,950 to stay sub-₱2,000 psychologically |
| ₱2,000–₱2,999 | "Premium Philippines" — feels like a real splurge | Sets and dresses can land here; must justify value clearly |
| ₱3,000 | **Third barrier** — near-Lululemon territory in PH context | Above ₱3,000 loses mass-premium shoppers; fine for dress/paddle bundles |
| ₱3,000–₱5,000 | "Imported brand prices" — Nike, Adidas territory | Only acceptable if product is clearly premium + technical |
| ₱5,000+ | "Lululemon / international luxury" | Lululemon PH ₱4,400+ bras, ₱6,640–₱7,360 leggings live here |

### Common PH Price Endings

- **₱X99** — most common; classic psychological sub-threshold (₱999, ₱1,499, ₱1,999)
- **₱X95** — secondary; slightly softer feel (₱995, ₱1,495, ₱2,495)
- **₱X,450 / ₱X,490** — mid-premium; feels "considered" not cheap (₱1,450, ₱2,490)
- **₱X,000** — round numbers feel more premium/confident (₱2,000, ₱3,000) — works for premium brands; risks "expensive" perception for mass
- **Bench approach:** ₱999.75 — hyper-specific to stay sub-₱1,000 optics; avoid for a premium brand

### Key Behavioral Observations

- **75% of PH consumers** cite price as a primary factor (imaRCgroup research)
- Comfort + quality + affordability are the top 3 attributes for PH women sportswear shoppers (Frontiers behavioral economics study, 2026)
- Aspirational vs. attainable: PH shoppers covet Lululemon but buy Recess — B Active's opportunity is in the gap
- COD preference (68%) means shoppers don't feel "spending pain" at checkout — reduces abandonment but increases RTO
- Average monthly income ~₱18,000: a ₱1,500 legging is ~8% of monthly income — meaningful but not prohibitive for ABC1 earners
- **Flash sale culture** is strong on Shopee/Lazada: brands that never discount are perceived as rigid; strategic ₱X99 sale events work well

## 8. KEY STRATEGIC NOTES

1. **The Recess gap is real:** Recess is relaunching (site password-protected May 2026) — B Active has a window to capture the "local premium pickleball/activewear" positioning while the lead local brand is offline.

2. **Bench Active is not a real competitor above ₱1,200:** Bench Active tennis skirts at ₱999–₱1,090 are functional but mass-market. B Active dresses at ₱1,690–₱1,990 serve a fundamentally different customer.

3. **Nike is the aspiration, not the competitor:** Nike PH tennis dresses at ₱3,495–₱5,795 set the ceiling. B Active at ₱1,690–₱1,990 feels like a "smart choice" vs. Nike.

4. **Pickleball paddle pricing is its own category:** Do not compete with PickleballPH on premium paddles (₱2,990–₱17,690). Consider a curated entry paddle at ₱1,490–₱1,990 positioned as "B Active's starter" for the sport-first customer.

5. **The ₱1,999 psychological threshold** is the most important ceiling for your core market. Keep dresses and sets sub-₱2,000 at launch, or only cross it with a clearly positioned "premium" SKU.

6. **Davao vs. Metro Manila:** Davao shoppers may be slightly more price-sensitive than Makati/BGC, but B Active's target (pickleball-playing women in Davao) skews ABC1 — they have the income and the motivation to pay for quality. Don't under-price for Davao.

7. **Free shipping threshold:** Set at ₱1,500. This encourages add-ons (a bra with a legging = ₱1,190–₱1,990, already near or over threshold) and minimizes the per-order shipping drag.
